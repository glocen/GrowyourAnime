# Taichi Panda 3: Dragon Hunter: a reference study

Read on 2026-11-24 by a research subagent (cloud session), from web text only: no footage was watched and no media was downloaded. The director named it as an inspiration. This study is for feel and design lessons only (`AGENTS.md`, rule 3): no name, character, creature, art, UI frame or sound of it is to be recreated, and nothing here is a source of numbers.

## What it is

- **Taichi Panda 3: Dragon Hunter** (Snail Games, October 2017; iOS, Android, PC): a free-to-play, open-world mobile MMORPG with two rival factions, dragon and beast hunting, and land and air mounts [1][2][3].
- The third game of a series. The first, **Taichi Panda** (2014-15), was a different shape: a dungeon crawler of short 2-3 minute stages with pets and action combat [9]. Where this study draws on the first game, it says so.
- Its updates kept adding collectables on top: equipment star upgrades to 120, wings, pet arrays, fashion, artifacts, mount skins, still shipping in January 2022 [2][4].

Labels: **SOURCED** (official copy, store listing, a critic's factual report), **COMMUNITY** (player guides, player reviews, the official forum), **OPINION** (a critic's or a player's judgement). Hosts that blocked or failed fetching are listed at the end; where only a search snippet was read, it says "snippet".

## Findings

### The loop and the session

- The loop is MMO standard: take a quest, kill N of a kind or collect N items, return, level, upgrade gear in menus. "Cut and paste quests" [6]. **SOURCED** (two critics agree [5][6]).
- A player guide's daily routine has eight steps (story quests, daily rewards, faction quests, dailies, a once-a-day 20-minute trial, eight dungeon runs of 15 minutes each with two others, then "whatever you enjoy"), and ends with parking the character on weak monsters in AFK mode overnight. About 1-1.5 hours a day [10]. **COMMUNITY**.
- Daily caps on experience and gear: one player says they make it impossible for a free player to catch up [7] (snippet); another says the level cap is reached within about two hours of play, which put new players off (snippet, search summary of player reviews). **COMMUNITY**.
- The first game: stages of 2-3 minutes, three stars for a fast clear, the best loot for three stars [9]. **SOURCED** (the developer's own words, a sponsored interview).

### Auto combat, and what the player still decides

- Tapping the current quest auto-paths the character to its next objective, and the same button fights for you. Pocket Gamer: "You don't need to engage with the battles at all, so why bother?"; verdict 2.5/5, for "those who just want to watch an MMO play itself" [5]. **SOURCED** (the behaviour) and **OPINION** (the verdict).
- Android Police: the map's quest markers are tiny and hard to find, which "forces you to use the all too convenient auto-play button". The game is "constant micromanagement in numerous menus" while the fight runs behind them [6]. **OPINION**, on a sourced observation.
- Players asked for it too: "We also hope that the game becomes more difficult to nerf auto battling so that the game isnt just played for us"; "Automated combat. You dont have to do anything." [7] **COMMUNITY**.
- What is left to the player: the class at creation, which skills to upgrade, combining skills well at higher levels, holding attack to fill an enemy's stagger bar, when to fire the mount's ability, and whether to enter enemy territory [8][10][11][12]. **COMMUNITY**.
- AFK mode has a target setting ("All" attacks every enemy in sight) [10]. **COMMUNITY**.

### Skills, combat feel, effects

- An orange stagger bar sits beside an enemy's health bar. Filled, it leaves the enemy "temporarily helpless" for a combo window. Mana comes back fast, so spells are meant to be used often [11]. **COMMUNITY**.
- Four skills on keys 1-4 (PC) or round the attack button (phone); the mount's ability is a tap on the mount's portrait, placed just above the attack and skill buttons [8][12][13]. **COMMUNITY**.
- The store copy promises "PC game quality 3D graphics", full-body motion capture, and blades, magic and fists [2] (snippet). The first game had elemental effects (fire, lightning, freezing), bosses that parry so they can't be knocked down, and stamina spent on skills and earned back by landing basic attacks [13] (snippet, fan wiki). **SOURCED** (the copy) and **COMMUNITY** (the wiki).
- **Gap:** no independent source judged how its skill effects read on a phone (scale, colour, clutter). Not measured here. If the director wants that lesson, it's a `/ga-reference-study` on footage, kept internal.

### Mounts, pets, dragons: collection

- Mounts can be tamed from common monsters up to world bosses; the launch copy said up to 1,000 of them, with random looks and special abilities, fought on the ground and in the air [3]. **SOURCED** (the developer's pre-launch copy).
- By 2018 mounts came mainly from breeding: two good mounts make an egg; hatch time grows with rarity; each mount breeds ten times. A guide calls it tedious and says to clear out common mounts early because the collection becomes hard to manage; breeding gives "between 0-100 mounts every day" [14]. **COMMUNITY**.
- Traits on mounts: of about 32 traits the guide ranks, 17 are "Bad"; only the "Special" ones matter in PvP. The first temper (reroll) each day is free and every temper after doubles in cost; hunting tickets come from spins and Bound Diamonds [15]. **COMMUNITY**.
- Pets: battle pets fight on their own, evolve with soul stones, and players liked having "so many cool mounts and pets"; another complains a pet's souls can take "more than 10 tries to obtain" [7][16]. **COMMUNITY**.
- Dragons, despite the title, are a theme and a world-boss target; no source describes a dragon system of its own [1][3]. **SOURCED** (by absence, weak).
- What players liked most, besides the graphics: "Playing with & collecting the various mounts is fun." [7] **COMMUNITY**.

### Gear and power

- Power is a single "might" number built from gear upgrades (forging with iron, the forge's cost rising steeply by level), star upgrades, wings, artifacts, mounts and pets [2][14]. A guide notes some might gains don't improve combat much [14]. **COMMUNITY**.
- Android Police: in-app purchases up to $99.99 an item, six store sections, most items not buyable with earned currency, XP booster packs that favour big spenders: "a perpetual money machine disguised as a game" [6]. **SOURCED** (the prices) and **OPINION** (the verdict).
- Players: "If you dont pay to win, then you stand no chance"; "PVP is forced at level 90" [7]. An ex-spender on the official forum says higher VIP levels only raised purchase limits while prices climbed (snippet; the forum would not load) [17]. On the first game: "it took a lot of money to be a top contender", "Dont put money into the game even if you like it"; one player says careful building still works without paying [18]. **COMMUNITY**.

### World and art direction

- One seamless map, no loading screens; one faction's capital Eastern in flair, the other Western medieval; a world said to be 150 times the first game's [1][2][3]. **SOURCED**.
- The looks were its best review: "probably the best looking MMO on Android", and in the same piece "a poor reproduction of a better game" [6]. Pocket Gamer: the world is "an empty expanse that offers no benefits to those who head off the beaten path" [5]. **OPINION**.
- Small location tasks ("adventures": shoot lightning at approaching creatures, hammer critters into the ground) give experience and are easy to miss [12]. **COMMUNITY**.

### UI on a phone

- Main menu: Character, Equipment, Hunting, Story, Settings; red dots mark what's new; a bonus icon by the minimap lists free rewards that refresh every few hours; equipped gear on the left, the new item on the right [8][11]. **COMMUNITY**.
- The critics' main UI faults: "a myriad of menus", and quest markers too small to tap [5][6]. **OPINION**.
- Against that, a long-time player of the first game: "We like the simplistic menu options. They dont over kill it." [18] **COMMUNITY**.

### Onboarding

- No forced tutorial; about 30 minutes of story quests brings a player to level 27 with a horse and a griffin [12]. Early experience "feels astronomical" and a flying mount comes early, which Pocket Gamer counts in its favour [5]. **COMMUNITY** and **OPINION**.
- Letting the game pick the faction pays a bonus of gold and premium currency [8][11]. **COMMUNITY**.

### Why players stayed, why they quit

- Stayed: the graphics, collecting mounts and pets, guilds and friends over years [7][18]. **COMMUNITY**.
- Quit: lost progress, pets and currency after updates or logins; login failures; server merges and cheating; being killed by other players on login; repeated quests; spending pressure [7][18]. **COMMUNITY**.

## Borrow as principle

Each in our own expression; none of its names, creatures, layouts or art.

1. **The world's enemies are the collection.** Its best-loved system turns monsters met in the field into things you keep. Ours already does it with shards dropped by Shades and keepers (`docs/north-star.md`, "Find"). Push the link: a shard should say which ground or keeper it came from, so a player remembers where they met that Voidkin. Tier 1 as copy on the shard card; anything more is tier 2.
2. **A companion's one big move lives on its own portrait.** One tap on a face, beside the action, is the clearest phone control it has. When manual signatures come (`docs/polish-later.md`), the lead's card is where the press belongs, and its cooldown can be read on the same frame today, while it fires by itself.
3. **A visible break bar invites watching.** Its stagger bar beside enemy health makes a combo window something you see coming. Our heavy stagger (`docs/design.md`, "States in a fight") could show its fill on the target's card, so a Haymaker landing past half reads as the payoff. Tier 2 (`docs/ui.md`, top centre).
4. **The first half hour gives something you can feel.** Its early experience and early flying mount are the one thing even its harshest review praised. Our first session already aims there; keep the second Voidkin, the power coming on and the first forge rank all inside it.
5. **Short runs with a clear grade.** The first game's 2-3 minute stages graded by speed fit a phone session. Our grounds and Tower floors should each be finishable in a few minutes, with a result a player can read at a glance (`docs/design.md`, "The Festival of Battles"). Any new grade is tier 2.
6. **Looks open the door.** Its art was what everyone praised first. Our "looks and sounds expensive" pillar is right, and its fate says why looks alone are not enough (Avoid 1).

## Avoid

1. **An auto mode that removes every decision.** Its critics' core complaint, echoed by players. Auto is fine (ours is the design), but the player must keep real choices in it: where they stand (the leash), one more pack or home (health carries), which lead, which build, when to take a keeper. If a run has no decision left, it's a screensaver. Test: in a blockout, can the player name one choice they made in the last minute?
2. **Auto-pathing standing in for a readable world.** Its tiny markers pushed players to the auto button and its world was empty off the path. Our grounds are small and should read by sight: a landmark, a lantern line, a keeper's ring seen from afar, never a "Go" button doing the walking.
3. **Collection bloat and chores.** A thousand mounts, breeding ten times each, clearing commons out by the hundred: a list, not a cast. Our first pillar (every Voidkin is somebody) is the opposite; keep the roster small and keep duplicates meaningful, never a sorting job.
4. **Dead options in a roll.** Seventeen of about 32 mount traits were rated "Bad". When traits come to Voidkin (`docs/polish-later.md`), every trait must be something a player could want.
5. **Paid power and paid randomness.** XP boosters, $99.99 items, VIP tiers that only raise purchase limits, daily caps that keep free players behind, rerolls doubling in cost, tickets bought with premium currency. All banned or against our rules already (`docs/design.md`, "What's sold"; `docs/roblox-rules.md`). Also avoid the softer form: a daily cap on experience or gear.
6. **A daily checklist an hour long.** Eight chores before "whatever you enjoy". Our base running offline is the right answer; dailies, if any, should be few and part of play.
7. **Forced PvP and griefing.** "PVP is forced at level 90" and being killed on login drove players away. Our Clash stays opt-in, and the Strip never lets one player hurt another's lead.
8. **Lost progress.** Lost pets, currency and accounts are the most common reason given for quitting. Our rule that fighters are valuables, decided on the server once, is retention, not just safety (`AGENTS.md`, "Data and code").
9. **Menu management as the game.** Its fights ran behind a stack of menus. Our quiet HUD and the Voidkin screen should keep upgrades to a few taps, with the fight in view when it matters.
10. **A power number that lies.** Its "might" rose on gains that barely changed fights. Our Combat Power's rule ("It never lies", `docs/design.md`) is the fix; keep it.

## Top 5 for us

1. **Auto combat must keep a decision every minute** (stand, push on or go home, pick the lead, take the keeper), or it becomes "an MMO that plays itself". Judge every blockout by it.
2. **Enemies are the collection:** the shard says where it came from, so a Voidkin carries a memory of a place.
3. **Never paid power, never paid randomness, never daily caps that keep free players behind:** the main reason Taichi Panda 3 was called pay-to-win.
4. **A small roster of somebodies, not a thousand of something:** no breeding chores, no bulk clearing, no dead traits.
5. **Reliability is retention:** lost progress was the top quit reason, so server-decided ownership and no-dupe saves are a player-facing feature.
