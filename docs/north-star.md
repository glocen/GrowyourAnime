# North star

What Grow your Anime is, what it must feel like, and the bar. One page. Read it before any piece players see. The systems are in `docs/design.md`, the numbers in `docs/balance.md`, the screens in `docs/ui.md`.

## The game

A stylish, slightly mature anime-inspired collection and action RPG on Roblox. You break **Void Shards** open in your base's **Void Breaker** and uncover **Voidkin**: original fighters, each with a type, a class, a look and a signature of its own. One of them leads and fights for you through the **Festival of Battles**. Up to six work in your **extraction bays** and make the **electricity** the Breaker runs on. Everything they win goes into an account-wide gear build that makes every Voidkin you own stronger.

It runs while you're away, it lives in a neighbourhood of eight bases, and it has a place where your strongest Voidkin fights everyone else's.

## The four pillars

1. **Every Voidkin is somebody.** A name, a silhouette, a way of fighting. Nobody is a recolour. A Common is a character, not filler.
2. **The build is the game.** Stats are deep and they add up honestly: no stacked multipliers, no number that runs away. Two players at the same power can build differently and the fight shows it.
3. **The base is alive.** Workers at their bays, the Breaker humming, a shard cracking while you fight. Electricity is what ties collecting to fighting.
4. **It looks and sounds expensive.** Hits land. Reveals are events. The HUD is quiet until something matters. Nothing looks like a placeholder in a public build.

## The bar: premium

The director, 2026-11-11: "let just deliver a premium game experience, next button was more of a side project to train we shipped it fast but it wasnt premium, we need to be even thoughtfull with the HUD".

- A piece players see ships when it's at the bar, never to meet a date.
- Fewer things, each finished. A system that can't be finished for launch waits for an update (`docs/production-plan.md`, "The cut line").
- The bar is judged from the player's camera, on a phone in landscape first.

## The first session

Not a stopwatch. The first session has to make a new player want the second one, and it does that by being good, not by being fast.

| Beat | What the player does | What they feel |
|---|---|---|
| Arrive | stands in their own base, the Breaker lit, three shards waiting | this place is mine |
| Choose | picks one of three starter shards and watches it break | a real reveal, a real character, a name |
| Fight | walks onto Lantern Row and their Voidkin fights beside them | it hits hard and it's on my side |
| Find | the first enemy drops a shard with a name on it | I want to know who's inside |
| Work | puts the second Voidkin in a bay and the power comes on | the base runs because of who I found |
| Build | the first piece of gear, the first forge rank | I'm getting stronger and I can see why |
| Want | sees what's next in this game: the Lamplighter on Hard, the Tower's door, the shop's featured stock | there's a lot here |

Nothing in the first session waits more than about half a minute, asks for Robux, or explains with a wall of text.

## What it isn't

- Not a farm. Nothing is planted, watered or harvested.
- Not an idle game with a skin. The lead fights in the world, in real time, beside the player.
- Not a gacha that sells luck. Nothing random is sold at launch (`docs/roblox-rules.md`).
- Not a copy of anything. Genre and general mechanics are free; every name, face, move and frame is ours (`AGENTS.md`, rule 3).

## How we'll know

- A new player who finishes Lantern Row comes back the next day.
- Two players compare builds and argue about which is better.
- A screenshot of a reveal looks like an advert.
