# Characters and worlds: what makes a cast and a map memorable, and where ours stands

Research subagent, 2026-11-24 (cloud session). Asked for by the director: inspiration for characters and worlds from Roblox anime hits and from anime, because the project risks drifting into "a pile of nonsense". Read first: `docs/roster.md`, `docs/north-star.md`, `docs/design.md` ("The Festival of Battles", "The world, in grey"), `docs/research/similar-games.md` (not repeated here), `docs/ip-list.md`, and the director's words in `docs/decisions.md` of 2026-11-15, 11-16 and 11-23.

**Labels.** SOURCED: a page I read, linked. SNIPPET: only the search engine's summary of the page was reachable, so it's weaker. COMMUNITY: what players or guide sites say. OPINION: mine. Nothing here was tested in play. Everything is structure to learn from, never names, looks or moves to take (`AGENTS.md`, rule 3).

**Status:** a proposal. Nothing here changes a decision. The world pitches touch the game's identity and the roster, so they are for research's register and the director's word (tier 3 where they replace grounds 2 to 6).

## Part 1: what Roblox anime hits do

### The field, late 2026

- Blox Fruits about 278K playing, Jujutsu Shenanigans about 133K, The Strongest Battlegrounds about 86K, all July 2026 snapshots (SNIPPET, [earnaldo](https://earnaldo.com/blog/best-roblox-games-2026)). Anime Vanguards: about 66.6K on 2026-10-12, an all-time peak of 140K on 2025-11-02, over 215K at its 2024 launch (SNIPPET, [RoWatcher](https://rowatcher.com/games/5578556129)); reported to have passed Blox Fruits in earnings (SNIPPET, [Beebom](https://beebom.com/anime-vanguards-surpasses-blox-fruits-top-earning-roblox-game)). No November 2026 chart was found: these are snapshots, not today.

### How their units become memorable

1. **Borrowed recognition does most of the work.** Anime Vanguards' starters are three famous protagonists (COMMUNITY, [FandomWire](https://fandomwire.com/its-a-cheap-attempt-to-copy-anime-adventures-anime-vanguards-has-the-potential-to-become-huge-but-fans-have-some-complaints-against-it/)). The Strongest Battlegrounds hides its characters behind titles ("Hero Hunter", "Destructive Cyborg") that fans decode at once (COMMUNITY, [rblxguide](https://rblxguide.com/games/the-strongest-battlegrounds/updates/character-real-names)). A player knows who a unit is before the reveal ends. **We can't borrow that, so we have to build it:** with archetypes and story beats every anime fan already knows (OPINION, the main finding of this page).
2. **A title as well as a name.** The Strongest Battlegrounds' "title instead of a name" means an epithet: a role in two words that a kid can repeat and a friend understands (SNIPPET, same source; the reading is OPINION).
3. **Rarity is shown, then shown louder.** Sol's RNG plays a bigger cutscene the rarer the roll, and a very rare roll is announced to the whole server (SNIPPET, [AllThingsHow](https://allthings.how/sols-rng-aura-tier-list-every-rarity-and-drop-rate-ranked/)). Anime Vanguards has a shiny variant, 1.5% on Mythic or higher (SNIPPET, [Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/roblox-news/how-get-shiny-secret-units-anime-vanguards)). Steal a Brainrot gives its top tier a rainbow name (SNIPPET, [Beebom](https://beebom.com/all-brainrots-in-steal-a-brainrot/)). The pattern: **the reveal escalates by tier, and the top tier gets a mark the others never have.**
4. **The names spread when they're fun to say.** Some Sol's RNG auras are popular for how they look; others because their names fit the game (SNIPPET, AllThingsHow, above). Steal a Brainrot's whole cast is sayable nonsense, which children repeat (OPINION).

### How their worlds are paced

- **Blox Fruits:** three seas, each with its own islands, level caps, NPCs and bosses; a new sea is a power spike; any island can be visited, but an under-levelled player is "destroyed" (SNIPPET, [Pocket Tactics](https://www.pockettactics.com/blox-fruits/maps), [Dot Esports](https://dotesports.com/roblox/news/blox-fruits-map-all-locations-and-npcs-for-the-first-second-third-seas)). Its sea structure loosely follows its source manga's story (SNIPPET, same). **Lesson:** every region is a different *kind* of place, not a recolour, and the next one is visible before it opens.
- The tower-defence wave of games is **criticised for sameness:** "the exact same or slightly different assets for their units, maps and even the entire UI" (SNIPPET, [Pro Game Guides](https://progameguides.com/roblox/anime-rangers-x-content-deleted-did-dmca-strike-again/)). Anime Vanguards was called "a cheap attempt to copy" its predecessor, with "no innovation" in units or designs (COMMUNITY, FandomWire, above). Copyright takedowns hang over the genre: Anime Rangers X was removed in April 2025, its cause disputed (SNIPPET, same).

### What players praise and complain about

- **Praise:** broken, dominant units that are fun to own; the feeling of a successor to a loved game (COMMUNITY, FandomWire).
- **Complaints:** sameness, no free premium currency or rerolls, a fixed starter choice, and heartbreak when an invested unit is power-crept by a new one (COMMUNITY, FandomWire). The last one matters for us: a Voidkin players love must stay worth using (`docs/balance.md`'s job; flagged, not solved here).

## Part 2: what anime and anime-styled games teach

### An original cast that sticks

- **Silhouette first.** Valve began every Team Fortress 2 character with its silhouette; proportions, weapon and clothing folds were chosen so each is unique, and readable with no inner detail at all (SOURCED, [Valve, GDC 2008](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2008/GDC2008_StylizationWithAPurpose_TF2.pdf); [Mitchell et al.](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2007/Mitchell-IllustrativeRenderingInTF2(Siggraph07%20Course%20Notes).pdf)). Overwatch's team asked of each new hero whether its silhouette looked too much like an existing one, and exaggerated attributes so a hero reads "at a distant glance" (SNIPPET, [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/how-torbjrn-became-the-art-inspiration-for-all-of-overwatch)). Skins never change a silhouette; they change the palette (SNIPPET, [Blizzard](https://overwatch.blizzard.com/en-gb/news/23537427)).
- **One colour block can carry a character.** One critic credits much of Tracer's readability to one bold splash of colour (OPINION of [Coelho-Kostolny](https://medium.com/@xavierck/character-readability-in-team-fortress-2-and-overwatch-68c41d454465)).
- **Personality before costume; faction in the costume.** Zenless Zone Zero's producer: the team starts from personality and background, costumes reflect the character's faction "so they aid recognition", outfits follow modern clothing that fits the city, and non-human members (bears, wolves, robots) were added because an all-human cast was less interesting (SNIPPET, [Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/zenless-zone-zeros-flashy-character-designs-are-a-result-of-the-dev-team-not-wanting-to-settle-on-just-one-character-creation-method-it-would-only-limit-our-ability-to-create-intere/), [Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/interview-preparing-zenless-zone-zero-2-0-and-its-characters/); the page itself was cut short when fetched).
- **Know the character, then draw.** Supergiant's writer says Jen Zee needs to understand a character deeply before its look, and her designs then sharpen the voice (SNIPPET, [MCV/Develop](https://mcvuk.com/business-news/behind-the-art-of-hades-we-value-artistic-integrity-and-excellence-in-artistic-craft-at-supergiant-however-were-first-and-foremost-a-game-design-lead-team/)).
- **Archetype plus twist** (OPINION, from the genre as a whole): anime casts are built from roles fans already know (the cocky rookie, the stoic guardian, the rival, the strict class rep, the cheerful glutton, the gentle giant) and made someone by one contradiction. The archetype gives instant recognition, the twist gives the name a reason to be remembered. This is how we replace borrowed recognition (Part 1, point 1).

### A world that holds together

- **A real reference under a fictional place.** HoYoverse: "a completely fictional fantasy world … however, we need references for such a creation" (SNIPPET, [Yahoo/Genshin](https://malaysia.news.yahoo.com/genshin-impact-completely-fictional-fantasy-world-not-realistic-154715370.html)); each region has its own culture and architecture, and a first landmark at its gate (Liyue's karst-arch gate, SOURCED, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liyue_(Genshin_Impact))).
- **One landmark per space, seen from the entrance.** Disney's "weenie": a visual magnet, clear from every major entrance, partly hidden along the path to reveal it in stages, stronger than everything round it (SOURCED, [Theory of Theme Parks](https://theoryofthemeparks.blogspot.com/2015/08/wayfinding-in-themed-design-weenie.html)); the same idea guides Mario Odyssey's levels (SNIPPET, [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/what-mario-learned-from-mickey-mouse---part-3-decision-making-and-weenies)).
- **Story told by the place.** Smith and Worch: stage the space so the player connects details into a story themselves; small self-contained vignettes in the set dressing (SNIPPET, [Nieman Storyboard](https://niemanstoryboard.org/2011/01/14/harvey-smith-on-environmental-storytelling-and-embedding-narrative/), [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1012647)).

## Part 3: our cast and worlds, frankly

### The cast (OPINION)

- **Strong:** the system under it is clean: five types, five classes, four elements, every role among the Commons. The signatures are readable actions (Haymaker, Scarf Step, Ground Wire). The Look rules of 2026-11-15 (fashion not a job, hair is the identity, an anime face, the element as a motif) are the right rules and match what Valve, Blizzard and HoYoverse do.
- **The table still fights those rules.** About twenty of the thirty-one Looks are a job or a costume: street brawler, courier ninja, apprentice, field medic, site worker with a door, tea-house cook, loader mech, bell knight, rail-yard brawler, sky courier. `docs/roster.md` already says such rows are designed again at their sheets; until then the table is what everyone reads, and it reads as a job fair.
- **No through-line.** The Roots span about twenty-five unrelated genres: steampunk, cooking manga, desert adventure, naval mecha, gothic knight, virtual singer, dragon knight. Nothing ties a desert dancer and an organ-pipe mech to a lantern festival or to the Void. This is the "pile of nonsense" the director fears, and it's in the data, not only in the art. Zenless Zone Zero solves it with factions; we have a Zone column that could do the same job and currently doesn't.
- **Names.** Most low-rarity names are short and readable (Rook, Kirra, Pip, Watt, Juno, Wren). Six are object nouns that make a person sound like a thing (Brick, Patch, Gantry, Gauge, Mallow, Dirge), against pillar 1, "A Common is a character". Several top names are hard for a ten-year-old to say or spell (Halcyon, Ironhymn, Stellane, Umbriel, Aegir). A chase name may be grand, but it should be sayable aloud on the first try.
- **Two colour systems on one body.** Type has five colours (the gems, the Shades' outlines) and element has four, and they cross: Mirage is a Blue type with Fire. At the game's camera, which colour is the character? Proposal below: type lives in the UI, element and hair own the body.
- **Five mechs on their own taller rig** are a second genre and a second production line beside the director's bar of 2026-11-15 ("real anime" casts). Keep them few and late; Aegir is the strongest of them.

### The worlds (OPINION, with the director's words)

- **Lantern Row works,** and its Shades (festival-goers the Void filled, a mask that falls) are a good fiction: a fall frees a person, which is gentle and fits rule 4.
- **The other five grounds are the same festival five times:** Drum Court, Mask Parade, Kite Heights, Ember Stalls, Mirror Pavilion, each "a recolour and rearrangement of one festival kit". The director has twice pulled the other way: "lantern ground is only for the first festival of battle ground" (2026-11-16) and "dont make the whole game about lantern drum festival … we make an anime game!" (2026-11-23, said of the masks on the base; its scope is the masks, so applying it to the grounds is research's reading, for the director to confirm). Blox Fruits' lesson is the same: each region a different kind of place.
- **The idea that fixes both: the Arcs are anime arcs.** The director already named the grounds "Arcs" (2026-11-19). Every anime fan knows the arcs of a long series: the festival episode, the training arc, the tournament arc, the school arc, the city-at-night arc. Lantern Row becomes **the Festival Arc**, and keeps all its work. Each later Arc is another genre beat the Void has walked into, with its own landmark, enemy family and keeper as the arc's villain. This gives kids and teens the borrowed-recognition effect of Part 1 with nothing borrowed: an arc structure belongs to the whole genre. Each Arc is also a Voidkin's home (below), which ties the cast to the map. Cost: grounds 2 to 6 of `docs/roster.md` are rewritten; none is built, so no work is lost; the Shades' one rig stays, re-dressed per Arc.

### Three world pitches

1. **Neon Ward, the Midnight Arc.** A city district at two in the morning, after rain: wet asphalt, rooftops joined by fire escapes and water towers, signs in an invented script. **Landmark:** a broadcast tower at the far end with a Void tear at its tip, seen from the gate and lost behind buildings on the way, then filling the sky at the keeper's roof. **Palette:** navy asphalt and black, magenta and cyan neon, one hot yellow for anything important (keeper, loot), clearly not Lantern Row's warm-white night. **Enemy family:** commuters and night-shift workers the Void filled, each with a glitching visor of static in place of a mask; the elite is a crowd of them moving as one; the keeper, **the Signal**, speaks through every screen in the street. **Music:** fast drum and bass under a lo-fi city-pop melody, the sub-bass dropping out when the tower comes into view. **Home of** urban fighters: hunters, couriers, street kids (Noct, Kirra).
2. **Cloud Peak, the Training Arc.** A mountain sect above the clouds: stone stairs that switch back up the cliff, bridges of rope and plank between pinnacles, practice yards with wooden posts, pines bent by wind. **Landmark:** a great bronze bell at the summit, its rope snapped, ringing on its own every few seconds, so it's heard before it's seen. **Palette:** cloud white, slate, pine green, one cinnabar red on gates and the keeper. **Enemy family:** disciples the Void filled, in practice robes with blank paper talismans over the face; the Dasher is a spear student, the Support beats a drum; the keeper, **the Head Instructor**, fights in three lessons, a new rule each third of its health. **Music:** a bamboo flute and hand drums over a hard rock beat, the bell as the downbeat. **Home of** martial fighters: Crane, Tumble, Halcyon.
3. **Clocktower Academy, the School Arc.** A boarding academy on a sea cliff at dusk after the last bell: a run through the courtyard, a long corridor of lockers, the gym, then the roof. **Landmark:** the clock tower, its minute hand snapped off and its face cracked open, violet Void light pouring out; seen from the gate, and climbed for the keeper. **Palette:** dusk orange and chalkboard green, school-uniform navy, cherry-blossom pink drifting, the Void's violet at the clock. **Enemy family:** students and club members the Void filled, each wearing a hollow armband that glows their type's colour; the Guard is a kendo-club shield-bearer, the Support a cheer squad; the keeper, **the Council President**, who "keeps order" by freezing the hall. **Music:** J-rock guitar and a school-chime motif turned into the hook. **Home of** school fighters: Juno, Lyric.

Held in reserve: **a Tournament Arc** (an underground bracket in a floodlit stadium, packs as rounds, the keeper the reigning champion). It's the most hype-friendly beat of all, but it overlaps the Clash Grounds, so it waits until the arena's identity is set.

### Character design rules (proposed, on top of `docs/roster.md`, "The look")

1. **The one-line pitch is an archetype and a twist,** written before any drawing: "the stoic guardian, who is the youngest in the cast". The twist must show in the idle or the signature.
2. **Each Voidkin has a home Arc,** and its wardrobe speaks that Arc's language (the Zone column becomes this). A newcomer can tell a Neon Ward fighter from a Cloud Peak one before reading a name.
3. **The silhouette test, at the game's camera, in black:** the five Voidkin of a ground side by side, filled black; each is named from its shape alone. One dominant shape breaks the R6 box (hair, a coat, a scarf, a weapon), never two that compete.
4. **One colour signature on the body,** about 60/30/10: the clothing's base, the hair, and the element's accent. The type colour lives in the UI (gem, card frame, the Shades' outlines), not on the body, so the two systems never fight.
5. **An epithet with the name,** shown on the reveal card and the profile: "Rook, Last Round". Two or three words, the role in plain speech, the thing a kid says to a friend.
6. **Names a ten-year-old can say on the first read.** One or two syllables up to Rare; longer allowed at Epic and above, but spelled as it's said. A person is never named after an object, unless the epithet makes it a nickname. Checked against `docs/ip-list.md` and a web search at the sheet.
7. **Personality in three seconds:** an idle with one habit, a pose on the reveal, and one short line on the card in the character's voice. No lore paragraph.
8. **The reveal escalates by rarity,** and Mythical gets one mark no other tier has (a server-wide line, a light only it carries), per Part 1, point 3. What it looks like is `docs/research/chest-opening.md`'s and the Breaker's piece, not this page's.

### Example concepts (original, to show the rules; for the sheet, not the roster)

- **Kade, "Last Train"** (Neon Ward; Common Assassin, Storm). *Rookie street kid who is polite to a fault: he bows after every knockout.* An oversized reflective rain jacket, hood up, to the knees, one sleeve knotted at the waist; white hair spiking out of the hood with one cyan streak. A pair of short tonfa wrapped in reflective tape that spark on contact. Idle: checks a cracked phone, sighs, pockets it. Signature: a sprint through the target, the jacket's reflective strip leaving a light trail. Silhouette: the long hooded jacket.
- **Yori, "Red Thread"** (Clocktower Academy; Rare Mage, Void). *The strict class rep who breaks every rule once a fight starts.* Blazer worn over one shoulder like a cape, a skirt with one long pleated panel, hair in a straight blunt cut with one braid; a long red cord wound up her arm that unspools into shapes in the air (the red thread is a folk motif across East Asia, no one work's). Idle: straightens her armband, glares. Signature: the cord draws a circle on the ground and pulls every enemy in it together. Silhouette: the caped blazer and the cord's loops.
- **Bao, "Cloudkick"** (Cloud Peak; Uncommon Warrior, Frost). *The cheerful glutton who is the sect's hardest kicker.* Rolled sleeves, wide trousers bound at the shin, three white scarves layered at the neck that stream behind her like cloud; hair in one huge high bun with a chopstick. Fights with kicks only, her shins wrapped in frost-white cloth. Idle: eats a steamed bun, one bite, then puts it away for later. Signature: a rising kick that leaves a frosted cloud print in the air, which bursts. Silhouette: the bun and the streaming scarves.
- **Halden, "the Stopped Hour"** (Clocktower Academy; Legendary Warrior, Void). *A gentle tutor who was the school's top student for a hundred years.* A long dark coat with a clock-face embroidered at the hem, silver hair with one black lock, round spectacles pushed up into it; two thin swords shaped like a clock's hands. Idle: checks a pocket watch that has no hands. Signature: "Rewind": three cuts, then the same cuts replay backwards for a second hit. Silhouette: the coat's hem and the two unequal blades.

**Name check:** Kade, Yori, Bao, Halden, Neon Ward, Cloud Peak, Clocktower Academy, the Signal, the Head Instructor and the Council President are not in `docs/ip-list.md`, and no conflict turned up in this session's searches, but no dedicated name search was run. Each is checked at its sheet, as the rule says. A time-stop signature on a clock character was avoided on purpose: that pairing is too close to famous expression; "Rewind" is a replay, not a freeze.
