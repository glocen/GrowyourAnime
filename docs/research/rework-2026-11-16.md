## 1. Basic Attributes and their conversions

**OBSERVED** (the director's two Taichi Panda 3 captures, 2026-11-16, `build/reference/dragon-hunter/2026-11-16_attribute-page-*`, internal): five attributes (Strength, Agility, Intelligence or Wisdom, Stamina, Spirit), one of them marked "Main" for the class; Battle Attributes in two columns below; one plain row per stat.

**A name clash, to settle first.** Strength, Agility and Intelligence are already three of our five types (Agility beats Strength, and so on: `docs/design.md`, "Voidkin"), and Stamina is close to Endurance. "A Strength Voidkin with 900 Agility" reads wrong. Research's call: rename the attributes, since nothing players see uses them yet. The working names below are **Might, Finesse, Vigor, Spirit and Insight**. The other way is renaming the types, which touches the roster, the type ring and its icons. The director picks.

**DESIGN, the model.**
- **Five attributes, each with its own job, and one Main for each class.** Five beats four here because there are five classes and each gets its own Main. Each attribute feeds a different family of stats, so no two overlap in purpose.
- **Every point converts by one fixed row,** the same for every class. **The class's Main converts once more** by its class's own row. That's the class difference, and it's all of it.
- **A Voidkin's own points** are its class's shape × its rarity × G(level) × its stars, so they grow on the curve the rest of the game already uses. Gear adds points flat.
- **Everything adds.** No attribute multiplies another, nothing multiplies an attribute, and there are no percent-of-attribute bonuses. Combat Power reads only the resolved Battle Attributes, so a point is never counted twice: once as an attribute, once as what it gave.
- **No manual allocation at launch.** It's already so: skill points wait for the tree (`docs/design.md`, "Voidkin").
- **The level's own Regen stays** (4 × G a second, every class); Spirit adds to it. The Healer's ×1.5 moves into its Main.

**The conversions** (Battle Attribute points per attribute point; seeds in `tools/balance/attributes.py`, `ATTR`):

| Attribute | Its job | Every class gets |
|---|---|---|
| **Might** (was Strength) | raw power | Attack 1, Health 4 |
| **Finesse** (was Agility) | precision and evasion | Attack 0.4, Hit 1.2, Critical 1.6, Dodge 0.6 |
| **Vigor** (was Stamina) | toughness | Health 5, Defence 1, Block 0.3 |
| **Spirit** | resolve and recovery | Regen 0.06, Health 6, Critical Resist 4, Elemental Resist 3 |
| **Insight** (was Intelligence) | seeing through armour | Attack 0.4, Penetration 2.5, Hit 1, Health 2 |

**Intelligence stays, as Insight,** because it has a job no other attribute does: damage that goes through Defence (Penetration), and the Mage's elemental power through its Main. It needs no mana.

## 2. Class-specific scaling

| Class | Main | A Main point gives, on top | What it makes the class |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warrior | Might | Attack +1 | steady damage, no weakness |
| Tank | Vigor | Attack +0.5, Health +3 | its damage grows with its toughness |
| Assassin | Finesse | Attack +1 | high Critical and Hit: burst |
| Mage | Insight | Attack +0.8, Elemental Attack +0.25 | through armour, with its element (every Mage has one) |
| Healer | Spirit | Attack +0.7, Regen +0.04 | hard to crit, resists the elements, lasts |

The class shapes (each Main the largest; points at level 1, Common; ×G(level) after):

| Class | Might | Finesse | Vigor | Spirit | Insight | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Warrior | **53** | 13 | 40 | 13 | 13 | 132 |
| Tank | 32 | 15 | **62** | 21 | 18 | 148 |
| Assassin | 38 | **53** | 32 | 12 | 12 | 147 |
| Mage | 35 | 12 | 32 | 14 | **52** | 145 |
| Healer | 31 | 11 | 37 | **51** | 11 | 141 |

At level 60 a Mythical's figures run in the thousands (Might 53 × 22 × 1.5 ≈ 1,750), the scale of the reference.

**SIMULATED** (`py tools/balance/attributes.py`):
- **The classes stand as they do today.** Each class's total is solved so its mean edge against every class and spec equals today's. Class-against-class edges differ from today's by 0.03 at most, at levels 10 and 40.
- **Each class's Main is its best attribute, and no attribute is best for everyone.** At level 30, a point of every other attribute is worth 43% to 91% of the Main's (Warrior's Finesse is the lowest at 0.43). None is dead.
- **Spreading versus stacking:** stacking the Main beats spreading for its own class (Warrior +0.32 against +0.21), and spreading beats stacking any other attribute. So gear choice reads "my Main first, then what I lack".
- **Against the game:** a Shade's kill time moves −14% to −2% and health lost per kill +1 to +2 points across classes and grounds. Enemy figures are authored from the on-budget build (`sim.py`, section 3), so the build re-authors them and the targets hold exactly.
- **Combat Power, unchanged weights, read on the new figures:** the higher number wins 92% of same-level duels (97% where one side wins 10% faster). Today it's 97%. The weights are refit with the build (`sim.py`, section 16) to win back the 5 points.
- **What changes in the figures:** Health is 20–40% lower at level 1 and the identity ratings (an Assassin's Critical, a Mage's Penetration, a Healer's resists) make up the value. The ratings a Voidkin brings itself were 0 before.

**Gear and attributes (DESIGN):**
- **Weapons:** main stat Attack. Their rolled lines favour their class's Main.
- **Armour** (Helmet to Cloak): main stat a Battle Attribute fixed by the slot, as designed. Rolled lines come from the five attributes and the Battle Attributes.
- **Jewellery** (Amulet, Rings, Earrings): **main stat a Basic Attribute,** rolled at the drop. It's where the player chooses ("this ring is Finesse"), and presets carry it per class.
- **A line's price** is its Combat Power at the generic row: 5 to 6 a point for every attribute, so an attribute line and a Battle line of one budget are worth the same. Its Main makes it worth more to one class: that's the point of a Main.

## 3. Intelligence

It stays, renamed **Insight** (part 1). Without it the Mage has no Main of its own, and Penetration has no attribute. With it, each class has one Main, and each attribute's job is different.

## 4. How stats are written, and the Overview

**OBSERVED, today** (`src/client/UI/VoidkinScreen`, `Rules/Sheet.luau`): ratings shown as raw points, percents as percents, three groups (Core, Attack, Defence), two cells a row, no chances and no sources.
**OBSERVED, the reference:** its "Hit 150%" and "Crit 153% against Crit Resist 157%" are opposed stats, not chances: a Hit over 100% beats Dodge, it isn't a probability.

**DESIGN: what each kind of number shows.** No "%" is ever added to a rating.

| Kind | Stats | Shown as |
|---|---|---|
| Points | Health, Attack, Defence, Regen (a second), Elemental Attack, Speed, Poise | the number |
| Rating against a foe | Hit, Dodge, Critical, Block | its points, then **its chance against an even foe**, smaller: "Critical 1,240 · 23%" |
| Rating with its own effect | Penetration, Critical Resist, Elemental Resist | its points, then its effect: "ignores 18% Defence", "−12% their Critical chance", "−20% elemental damage" |
| Percent | Critical Damage, Critical Guard, Lifesteal, Damage Bonus, Damage Reduction | the percent, as today |

- **The even foe** is a fixed reference: a Voidkin of the viewer's level on the on-budget build (`balance.md`, "onBudget"), its ratings in Config. Every chance shown is exact against it, computed by the same function the hit uses (`Stats.Chance`), so the sheet can't drift from the combat.
- **No chance ever reads over 100%,** since each one is a probability with a ceiling (Dodge 40%, Critical 60%, Block 50%).
- **One caption under the heading,** "chances against an even foe", and nothing else. "Plain list, no taps" stands (the director, 2026-11-15).

**DESIGN: the Overview's hierarchy.**
1. **The header:** name, class, level and its bar. Combat Power stays at the feet.
2. **BASIC ATTRIBUTES:** the five in one row or two, the Main marked (a small "Main" tag, ours), each with its total.
3. **BATTLE ATTRIBUTES,** in three blocks:
   - **Core:** Health, Attack, Defence, Speed, Poise.
   - **Offence | Defence, paired on each row,** so what counters what reads without a word: Hit | Dodge; Critical | Critical Resist; Critical Damage | Critical Guard; Elemental Attack | Elemental Resist; Damage Bonus | Damage Reduction; Penetration | Block. The last pair isn't a counter pair: it's where both fit.
   - **Sustain:** Regen | Lifesteal.
- **What changes from the director's list:** Regen and Lifesteal leave Core and Offence for Sustain, since the Tower's carried health makes them one question; and the counter pairs sit side by side.
- **Numbers:** thousands separators, no decimals on points, one decimal on percents under 10%, none above.
- **Every upgrade shows as a change:** after an equip, each changed row shows its gain in green for a moment, as Combat Power does. On a phone the sheet scrolls in one column, its blocks' headings pinned.

## 5. The mobile inventory: palette and composition

Modulo leads the art (the director's words). Two or three directions come first, and none is built before the pick. Research's part is the usability brief each direction is judged against, and the verdict.

**The brief:**
- **One frame level.** No frame inside a frame. Panels part by tone and space, not borders, so the screen loses its bulk.
- **Two zones on a phone in landscape:** the collection (grid) and the selected item. They never compete: the item sheet slides over the Voidkin's half when an item is chosen, and closes back.
- **The grid:** cells about 64 pt on a phone, 5 or 6 across, the icon filling most of the cell. Rarity shows on the cell's edge and a corner mark, never as a full colour fill. An item that would raise Combat Power carries the up arrow. An item the selected Voidkin's class can't wear is dimmed and carries its class mark.
- **Categories:** one strip of icon tabs over the grid (All, Weapons, Armour, Jewellery, Chests, Materials), each 44 pt or more, with one sort toggle (Rarity, Power, New).
- **The item sheet:** the name and its Combat Power at the top (the director, 2026-11-15). Then the comparison against what's worn in two columns, with Basic Attribute changes, then Battle Attribute changes, then the Combat Power change, gains green and losses red. Equip sits at the right thumb. For an item of another class, "Warrior only", and Equip off.
- **Empty states:** a quiet line on where items come from. Never an empty frame.
- **The palette:** fresh, low-saturation, pleasant for long reading, with one accent. Rarity colours and the stats' colours are reserved for what they mean and never used as decoration. The director has already said "to much purple" once (2026-11-15).
- **Judged** from phone-sized captures: a full bag, long names, a comparison, a weapon of another class, an empty bag. On the Voidkin screen as a whole, the figure, its slots and the left rail stay one design.

## 8. The Tower

**DESIGN, the launch loop:** Fight, Win, Rise, Fight, Die, Rewards, Reset.
- **Behind each base, empty.** Its lot is bare but for a ring set in the ground where platform 1 will appear, so the player knows where to start. No standing stack and no silhouette (this replaces the sleeping Tower of 00:43).
- **A run:** the player steps into the ring with their lead. A circular platform rises with them on it, and an enemy Voidkin appears. The lead fights it automatically. On a win, a new platform assembles above, and the lead and the player's avatar leap up to it together. The avatar rides along, since the camera stays the player's (the director, 2026-11-14), so it follows them up with no control taken.
- **Enemies:** our own Voidkin in an enemy look: the same models, darkened with void light. No new models. Floor f stands at level 1 + (f − 1) ÷ 2, on a lighter build, with a little more than half a lead's health.
- **Health carries:** nothing heals between floors. In a fight, Regen and Lifesteal work, but **never more than 15% of the lead's health is healed in one floor** (seed).
- **Death:** the run ends, the platforms fall away as light, and the results screen opens.
- **Results:** highest floor this run (and the record, if it's beaten), opponents defeated, Credits, items and chests, other rewards. Rare rewards stand apart in their rarity's light.
- **Fast floors:** a floor far below the lead's power falls in one hit, and the rise quickens. The climb to where it gets hard takes seconds, not minutes. The model shows why this matters: a level-30 lead's first 30 floors are no contest.
- **Not at launch** (the director's words): milestone bosses, cash-outs, branches, extra currencies.
- **Rewards,** by floor won: Credits rising with the floor, a chest chance rising with it, and a first-time bonus past the record. **Every reward is written to the run's ledger on the server when its floor is won,** and granted once when the run ends. A run's id makes a repeated claim a no-op.
- **Interruptions:**
  - Leaving the ring, teleporting home, a disconnect or a server shutdown each end the run at its last floor won, and nothing won is lost.
  - On the next join, the results screen opens once from the ledger.
  - A run never resumes.
  - The lead and Regalia are frozen from the first fight to the end, as at a keeper (D10).
- **Performance:** at most three platforms stand: the current one and two below. Older ones fold into one thin column of rings, a single cheap mesh, so the height still reads.

**SIMULATED** (`py tools/balance/tower.py`, median of 60 runs):

| Level | Floors, a weak build (0.4) | on budget (0.7) | a full build (1.0) | Minutes on budget |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 3 | 5 | 7 | 0.3 |
| 15 | 17 | 20 | 24 | 1.1 |
| 30 | 40 | 50 | 58 | 2.5 |
| 45 | 57 | 69 | 78 | 3.3 |
| 60 | 75 | 90 | 101 | 4.3 |

- **Power shows as floors:** at level 30, a full build climbs 18 floors past a weak one. The five classes end within 3 floors of each other.
- **The heal cap changes nothing today:** floors are the same from 10% up. It's a guard for when Lifesteal is wired and heals grow.
- **Not known yet:** how long a run should last to feel good. The seed lengthens the hard end, not the easy start. Judged in play.

## 9. The generator and the machines' stages

**DESIGN:**
- **Stage I, Primitive:** machine levels 1 to 5. **Stage II, Developed:** 6 to 14. **Stage III, Advanced:** 15 and up.
- **Why those thresholds:** at launch's ceilings (2, 6 and 30 by the base's level), base level 2 opens Stage II in the first sessions, and Stage III is the goal after base level 3 (about 5 hours of play). Every stage lands as a moment: the machine changes in the world (`docs/design.md`, "The base").
- **The order:** the generator, then the Void Breaker, the bays, the battery and Training machine, and the rest.
- **The look,** with one question open to the director: last night's words made these void machines, never real power equipment, and today's ask "early-stage industrial, mature anime-industrial". Research's reading: **void machines built from parts you can read** (gears, pistons, cables, clamps) **doing void work.** Stage I is crude and exposed, and the void's light runs in visible paths. It's never a copy of a real power plant.
- **Modulo's 2 or 3 directions for the generator at Stage I,** each with a line on how it grows to II and III, are judged from the game's camera.

## 10. Class weapons and compatibility

**OBSERVED:** the pairs are already set by the director (`docs/design.md`, "Regalia"): Tank, Great Axe and Shield; Mage, Staff and Tome; Assassin, Dagger and Throwing Blades; Warrior, Sword and Short Sword; Healer, Crystal Baton and Codex. Class rules aren't built: the save holds `Preset.Slots` and the resolver adds every slot.

**DESIGN:**
- **The package's table matches ours but for the Warrior.** Spellbook is our Tome, and Sceptre and Tome are our Crystal Baton and Codex. The Warrior has two swords by the director's word of 2026-11-12, and the new example gives Sword and Shield. **Research's lean: keep two swords.** The Tank already holds the Shield, and a class's pair should be its own. The director picks.
- **The rules:**
  - Each weapon has one class.
  - Main Hand and Off Hand take only their class's pair.
  - A preset holds one pair per class, as designed.
  - **The resolver reads only the pair of the Voidkin's class,** so a wrong-class bonus can't apply even if a save were wrong.
  - The server checks every equip, and refuses a wrong class with a reason the screen shows.
  - Everything else (12 slots) suits every class.
- **Drops:** a weapon of any class may drop, and stays in the bag. Weapon drops lean toward the lead's class (seed: 60%, the rest among the classes the player owns), so a drop is rarely useless.
- **The comparison:** against what's worn in the same preset, for that weapon's class, or for the selected Voidkin when it's armour. It shows Basic Attribute changes, then Battle Attribute changes, then Combat Power. The server computes it with the resolver run twice, so it can't drift.
- **The weapons' look:** an icon for each kind, modelled in Blender and rendered (AR6's batch 3). Never seen on a Voidkin (the director, 2026-11-12 and today).

## 11. Chests

**DESIGN:**
- **Where they come from:**
  - **A Shade:** 5% a kill (seed). The first session's third kill always drops one, for the north star's "Build" beat.
  - **An elite:** one chest every time, one tier up.
  - **A keeper:** a better chest on the first win, a chest after.
  - **The Tower:** a chest chance rising with the floor.
- **A chest is named for its ground and tier** (a Common Chest of Lantern Row), and its table rolls the item's rarity, slot and lines on the server when it's opened.
- **The rarity targets,** with rates fitted to them by simulation at the build, from `killsPerHour` 450 and the grounds' pace:

| Rarity | First one, at about | Mostly from |
|---|---|---|
| Uncommon | the first hour | Lantern Row |
| Rare | 3 to 4 hours | grounds 2 and 3 |
| Epic | 10 to 15 hours | grounds 4 and 5 |
| Legendary | 35 to 45 hours | ground 6 and the Tower's deep floors |
| Dawn | rarer still | the last ground and the Tower (as designed) |

  At 5%, a player gets about 20 chests an hour, about 1,300 over the climb to level 60. That's why opening in bulk matters.
- **The convenience the director approved:**
  - Chests within about 20 studs of the avatar fly to it.
  - Identical unopened chests stack ("Lantern Row Common × 37").
  - **Open all** opens a stack in one server step and shows one summary, sorted by rarity, with rare items lit.
  - An Epic or better gets a short reveal of its own.
  - **Auto-salvage** is a setting: items under a rarity the player picks turn into forge stones and Credits on opening, never an item that would raise Combat Power.
  - The bag has a cap (seed 300) and says so before it fills.
- **Safety:**
  - Each open is one transaction with an id, so a repeat is a no-op and nothing is half-granted.
  - Every item is written in the same save step that removes the chest.
  - Bulk opens are chunked (seed: 50 per step) so one request can't stall the server.
- **Rule 4:** chests are earned by play. **No chest, key or drop-rate boost is ever sold for Robux or a currency bought with Robux** (`docs/roblox-rules.md`).

   4. Chests and bulk opening.
   5. The inventory on the chosen direction.
   6. The generator and the stages.
   7. The Tower.
   8. The responsive pass across every screen.

**The risks:**
- **Scope:** this is most of phase 3 at once. Each piece goes in as a block first, one at a time, and polish waits (`AGENTS.md`).
- **Combat Power's accuracy** drops to 92% until its weights are refit. That's a refit at the build, not a redesign.
- **The rename:** attribute names appear everywhere, so they're decided before any code.
- **The save's size:** hundreds of items per account. Mitigated by the bag's cap, auto-salvage, and items stored compactly. Faber measures it.
- **Re-doing AR16 and AR17:** a real cost. The grey trial keeps it to one re-lay.
- **Lifesteal isn't wired yet** (Hit returns it, FightService doesn't apply it). The Tower's heal cap is there before it is.
- **The Tower's reward rate** against the grounds' is checked in the earning model, so neither becomes the only place worth playing.

**The checks, each before its piece closes:**
- **Attributes:** logic cases for every conversion, every Main and the no-double-count rule (Combat Power from the resolved figure only). `attributes.py`'s reports pass their wanted lines: class edges within 0.05 of today's, each Main the best, no secondary under 0.4, Combat Power refit to 97% or better.
- **The stat sheet:** each shown chance equals `Stats.Chance` against the even foe, in a case. Never over 100%. Phone captures, long numbers included.
- **Class rules:** cases for a wrong-class equip refused, a preset applying only its class's pair, and a save edited by hand still not applying a wrong weapon.
- **Chests:** a bulk open of 1,000 in a case, every item granted once. A repeat request is a no-op. A server stop mid-open loses nothing and grants nothing twice.
- **The Tower:** cases for health carried, the heal cap, the ledger written each floor, a disconnect granting what was won, and a rejoin showing the results once. Platforms never more than three. Captures of the climb and the results.
- **The base:** the size tests in part 7, from the player's camera, on a phone.
- **The inventory:** QA's evidence pack once QA joins, research's pass, captures from a phone with a full bag, long names, a comparison, another class's weapon, and an empty bag.
