# TR73. Ranged against melee, measured in real fights: the duel matrix (research, 2026-11-27, 12:38; for Faber)

The director's word of 12:31 (`docs/decisions.md`): Pip seems to fire too fast and keep knocking melee back, so a lower-level Pip may beat a higher-level Rook. No nerf or buff before the interaction is measured in real combat, and the fix must keep each identity: ranged keeps a real range advantage, melee gets a fair chance to close. The method: the handbook's `design/balance.md`.

**What the code says (Codex's trace, `build/scratch/quaestor/codex/transcripts/2026-11-27_pip-rook.md`; research checked the starred lines in the code at this commit):**
- *Pip's combo: shots at 0.17, 0.43 and 1.03 s, again from 1.5 s, no cooldown, pushes 0.3, 0.3 and 1.0 studs, start within 14.5, hit within 15.5 with no travel time (`Definitions/Actions.luau`, PipCombo).
- *Every landed hit on an actionless lead renews a 0.28 s flinch after its hold, with no immunity between (`FightService.luau`, the hit response; `Config/Combat.luau`, Flinch). A Tower rival doesn't flinch.
- *Rook walks 16 studs a second (Warrior), with no dash or lunge, and starts his jab within 4.5 studs (`Config/Stats.luau`, Speed).
- *Pip's skip triggers inside 5 studs, every 2.5 s, when no swing at him is winding up (`Config/Combat.luau`, Skip). **Research's read:** that's wider than Rook's jab start at 4.5, so a walking Rook sets off Pip's escape before he can start a swing, whenever the skip is ready.
- On paper (Codex, MEASURED from the code's numbers), Rook still gains about 5 studs a second against a standing Pip. So the paper shows a slowed approach, not a lock. The real fight decides.
- Every term in the loop except damage ignores level: beats, pushes, flinch, walk, skip, ranges. Level changes only who dies first.

**The piece: a duel harness, dev-only, on the real FightService:**
1. Two fighters on a flat stage at Pip's engage distance, real AI and kits, a seed per run. Both roles each way: Pip as the lead against a Rook rival, and Rook as the lead against a Pip rival. The avatar stands still behind its lead.
2. Pairs: Pip and Lyric, each against Rook, Watt and Kirra. Level-matched at 30, then the ranged one 5 levels below. 10 runs each.
3. **Logged per fight,** to `build/captures/tr73/`: the winner and the time; the time to the melee's first swing started and first hit landed; the seconds at each range (under 6, 6 to 13, over 13); damage uptime per side; flinches taken and seconds flinched; pushes and their studs; skips; dodges.
4. **The same run with one rule off at a time,** through `Tune`: the skip, the flinch on leads, the pushes. This shows which term carries the effect.
5. One player-camera capture of the worst pair, for research's eye.

**Then research** reads the matrix, names the loop with its measured terms, and brings the director the options that keep both identities (Codex's three, among them: a flinch immunity after recovery, the skip held while a melee foe is in its start range, a short committed step into Rook's jab). No number changes before that.

**Also, from TR69 B (12:35):** a hit reaction shortened an elite's O2 countdown to 0.83 s. A warning should keep its full length from when it first shows. Check it in the same piece.

**Status:** requested, 2026-11-27, 12:38. Queued after Faber's Turing tools.

### Faber: the late first swing found and fixed; poise built at 0 (2026-11-27, 20:48; e267eb73)

- **The cause, MEASURED: the camera, not the rules.**
  - A rival coming at the lead from outside the camera's 55° arc makes the lead wait beside the avatar, up to `View.Wait` (3 s), before it walks in (TR24's fix 2). 3 s plus the walk is the 4 to 5 s.
  - The duel read Studio's real camera, and a character respawn turns it. So a batch's start hung on where the camera pointed, not on its rule.
  - In ×10's raw rows the late starts are the three off batches and all four Kirra batches (3.7 to 5.9 s), plus four of Watt against Lyric 30/25 (5.9 s). Rook and Watt against Pip mostly start at about 1 s. Rook against Lyric starts at 2.1 to 2.5 s; that may be partly the same wait.
- **The fix:** the duel fixes the camera's look from the lead to its rival for every fight (`FightService.FixView`, the bench only), the player watching their fight. Checked with Studio's camera pinned 90° off the rival: Kirra's first swing 1.03 to 1.63 s (was 4.5), Rook's with Skip off 1.16 s (was 5.5).
- **Found on the way, a live bug fixed:**
  - At a batch's end the pad was removed with the avatar and the lead on it. The lead fell past the kill height and fought on as a Fighter with no body: no swing in 60 s, pushed 56 times without moving.
  - The duel now brings the avatar home first.
  - `LeadService` rebuilds any lead whose body is gone, which also covers a lead that falls off a map in play. Tested by destroying the body: rebuilt in 0.88 s, health carried.
- **So every ×10 number with a late start is NOT VERIFIED:** the off table, Kirra's rows, and Watt against Lyric 30/25. Rook against Lyric may be too. The rows run again with the fix, below.
- **Poise, built and off:**
  - `Config.Combat.Poise.Lead` (seconds, 0) is the window in which a pushed lead takes no new push. The flinch stays, and a finishing throw still throws.
  - The rule is in pure Luau (`Rules.Fight.Pushes`, a logic case). The duel spec takes `Poise`, and each result says which it ran.
  - At 0 nothing changes in play.
- **Four facts:** implemented; tested (type check 0 new, logic offline 58,496 passed, the camera check and the rebuild above); nothing players see; not accepted.
